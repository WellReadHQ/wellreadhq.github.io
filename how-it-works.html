How it works

From your sensors to an early warning, inside your network.

Five steps, all on your own server. Your safety systems stay exactly as they are.

  1. 1

    Install on your server

    Linux (one command, or an offline bundle for sites without internet) or Windows (a click-through installer). A setup wizard in the browser loads your pilot licence, connects your company sign-in and creates the first admin.

  2. 2

    Connect read-only

    The Edge agent reads OPC UA, MQTT or OSIsoft / AVEVA PI Web API and passes readings to WellRead. CSV import builds a site from an export. Nothing is ever written back to the plant.

  3. 3

    Learn normal per instrument

    WellRead checks reading quality and learns each instrument's normal range from its own history. Before real data flows, a clearly labelled practice site with simulated data lets your team learn the screens.

  4. 4

    Grouped, prioritised alerts

    Limit breaches, fast changes and steady drifts toward a trip are raised early. Alerts on the same equipment are grouped into one episode; trips are raised at once.

  5. 5

    Operators teach it

    After each acknowledgement operators answer one quick question. Over the pilot, that feedback shows which alerts to tune, so the system gets quieter.

The safety promise

WellRead watches and advises. It never acts.

  • Read-only at the plant. WellRead only reads plant data (OPC UA, MQTT, PI Web API, CSV). It has no way to write to a PLC, DCS, valve, pump or setpoint.
  • Never touches trips. It never controls, delays, bypasses or replaces a SIS / ESD trip. Trips seen in good readings are shown at once and are never delayed, grouped away or suppressed. The plant's own safety system is always the safety layer.
  • A person decides. Every suggestion ("check the choke", "looks like gas lock") is advice for a person to confirm. Sensor data can suggest a shutdown or restart; only a person sets the operating state, and WellRead records who.
  • Bad data never raises an alarm. Bad or uncertain readings are stored as received and shown greyed, never filled in and never judged.
  • Stale data is shown as stale. When a feed goes quiet or freezes, its values are greyed and marked "No data" or "Frozen". This changes only what WellRead shows.
  • Suggestions are labelled. Estimates say "estimate", best guesses say "best guess, not a diagnosis", suggestions say "check" or "ask".
  • Your data stays with you. On your own server, readings, users and the audit trail stay in your own database and nothing is sent home. A pilot request on WellRead's site keeps only a random code, its status, the pilot length and the agreement version: no name, email or company.

Who uses it

Operator

Watches the map and the Alarms & alerts screen, acknowledges and closes alerts, and asks for an operating-state change.

Manager / supervisor

Follows sites and alarm history. Can be given rights to approve operating-state changes (a supervisor), or just view.

IT admin

Installs WellRead on the company server, connects the company sign-in (Microsoft Entra, Google Workspace, OIDC, SAML, Active Directory / LDAP), and manages users, the licence and updates.

From the plant to the screen

What happens to one reading, step by step.

  1. 1

    Read at the plant

    The Edge agent, a small program on a plant PC, subscribes to OPC UA values or MQTT topics, or reads PI Web API with read-only requests. Each value keeps its quality: good, uncertain or bad.

  2. 2

    Send out

    The agent sends readings to your WellRead server over HTTPS with that site's own key. The plant never opens an inbound port. If the link is down, readings wait on disk and are sent later, oldest first, with their original timestamps. A CSV upload in the app is another way in.

  3. 3

    Store once

    Each reading is stored once per tag and timestamp, so a replay never creates duplicates.

  4. 4

    Evaluate

    Rules run on good readings only: limits, rate of change and trend toward a trip, plus alarm management (the quiet Watch lane, grouped episodes, chattering sensors). Bad or uncertain readings never raise, escalate or close an alert.

  5. 5

    Watch the feed itself

    WellRead notices when a source goes quiet (after 60 seconds by default) or a value stays frozen too long. Those values are greyed with "No data" or "Frozen" and a "Data stopped" notice names the source. It is a warning to people only; nothing is sent to the plant.

  6. 6

    Show

    The app fetches fresh readings and alerts about every 60 seconds and draws the 2D map, the optional 3D view, trends and the Alarms & alerts screen, on phone and desktop.

  7. 7

    People act

    Operators acknowledge and close alerts and request operating-state changes; an admin or a supervisor approves, and nobody approves their own request. Every change goes to the audit trail.

On your own server

The same app runs inside your network. WellRead's public site only hosts the demo and pilot requests; your plant data never goes there.

  • Installed inside your network on Linux or Windows.
  • Sign-in only through your company's identity provider, plus one emergency admin login for when that provider is down.
  • Admins can require two-step sign-in, with one-time backup codes.
  • Plant links can use OPC UA encryption or MQTT over TLS; a new plant certificate is refused until an admin trusts it, and unencrypted links stay blocked until an admin knowingly allows them.
  • Daily encrypted backups of the database and settings, locked with a passphrase.
  • Every query is limited to your own company, and every configuration change is recorded in the audit trail.

How a pilot works

  1. 1

    Read the Pilot Agreement, tick "I agree" and pick 30, 60 or 90 days on the Request a pilot page.

  2. 2

    You get a random pilot code (WR-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX). That code is all that is kept about you; a code still waiting after 14 days is deleted.

  3. 3

    When the pilot is approved you receive a signed licence: a small text file holding only the pilot code and the end date. Your server checks it offline; nothing is sent home.

  4. 4

    After the end date there are 14 days of grace in which everything keeps working and admins see a renewal banner.

Glossary

The words you will meet in WellRead, one line each.

Plant and oilfield terms

SIS (Safety Instrumented System)
The plant's own automatic safety layer that shuts equipment down when a limit is crossed. WellRead never touches it.
ESD (Emergency Shutdown)
The system or action that safely stops a unit or the whole plant in an emergency. WellRead never triggers, delays or blocks it.
Trip
An automatic shutdown when a value crosses its trip limit. In WellRead a "trip" alert is the critical level, always shown at once.
Trip limit
The value at which the plant's safety system would shut the equipment down (for example PSHH, pressure safety high-high).
Alarm
A warning shown to people that something needs attention; in WellRead, an alert in the main list (as opposed to the quiet Watch lane).
Alert
WellRead's record of a rule firing on a tag (warning or critical), with a timeline of open, acknowledge and close events.
Watch lane
A quieter list for single-tag warnings that are not yet confirmed; they move to the main list if confirmed or if they last.
Episode
Alerts on the same site and equipment within a short time, grouped and shown as one card.
Acknowledge (ack)
A person confirms they have seen an alert. It does not change the plant.
Setpoint
The target value a control loop tries to hold. WellRead only reads values; it never changes setpoints.
Tag
One measured value from the plant (for example CDU.FEED.FLOW), with a unit, a normal band and warning and trip limits.
Normal band / warning limit
The range a tag should normally stay in; leaving it raises a warning.
Rate of change
How fast a tag moves per minute; a fast change can raise a warning.
Time to trip
WellRead's estimate of when a trending tag would reach its trip limit, labelled as an estimate.
P&ID
Piping and Instrumentation Diagram: the standard drawing of a plant's pipes, equipment and instruments. WellRead uses P&ID-style symbols for units.
Unit / asset
A piece of equipment (heater, separator, compressor, well) that tags belong to.
Site
A plant, pad or facility that holds units; classed upstream, midstream or downstream.
Upstream / midstream / downstream
Production (wells); transport and storage (pipelines, compressor stations); refining and processing.
Operating state
Running, Startup, Shutdown or Maintenance. Some low-side warnings are muted in non-running states; trips never are.
Wellhead / separator / CDU
The top of a well / a vessel that splits oil, gas and water / the crude distillation unit of a refinery.
MAWP
Maximum allowable working pressure of a vessel.
Historian
A plant database that records tag values over time (for example OSIsoft / AVEVA PI).
Reading quality
Good, uncertain or bad, as reported by the source. WellRead only evaluates good readings.
Stale data ("No data" / "Frozen")
A reading that stopped arriving or has not changed for too long. WellRead greys it and shows a "Data stopped" notice.

Connectivity terms

OPC UA
The industrial standard protocol for reading values from plant control systems. The Edge agent subscribes read-only.
OPC UA security mode
OPC UA's own signing and encryption (Sign / SignAndEncrypt), which the Edge agent can use.
MQTT
A lightweight publish / subscribe messaging protocol. WellRead subscribes to topics and never publishes to plant brokers.
Sparkplug
A common MQTT payload convention in industry; its quality values are understood.
PI Web API
The HTTPS interface of the OSIsoft / AVEVA PI historian. The Edge agent only reads from it.
AF element
An asset in PI Asset Framework whose attributes can be read as tags.
Edge agent
WellRead's small program on a plant PC that reads plant data and sends it out over HTTPS.
Store-and-forward
The Edge agent keeps readings on disk while offline and sends them later with their original timestamps.
Tag discovery
The Edge agent lists the tags it can see; an admin picks which ones to watch.
TLS
The encryption used for HTTPS and for MQTT over TLS.
Trusted certificate
A plant server's certificate an admin has approved; new ones are refused until then.

Using WellRead

Guest demo
A demo company a visitor can use without an account, replaying real public recordings; it is reset after use.
Practice site
A clearly labelled simulated site on your own server, for training before real data flows.
First-run guide
A short, skippable walkthrough for each role the first time someone opens WellRead; IT admins also get a setup checklist.
Pilot
A time-limited WellRead installation on one site; you pick 30, 60 or 90 days.
Pilot code
The random code (WR-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX) you get from "Request a pilot"; the only thing stored about you.
Pilot Agreement
The agreement you accept with a tick box before asking for a pilot. A new version asks everyone to accept again.
Licence
A small text file signed by WellRead holding only the pilot code and end date, checked offline on your server.
Grace period
14 days after a licence ends during which everything still works and admins see a renewal banner.
Identity provider
Your company's sign-in system: Microsoft Entra ID, Google Workspace, Okta, AD FS, Keycloak and others.
OIDC / SAML
The two standard web sign-in protocols WellRead supports with identity providers.
LDAP / LDAPS / AD
Active Directory sign-in over the directory protocol (LDAPS is the encrypted form), for plants without internet.
Emergency login
One admin login (recovery passphrase plus authenticator code) for when the identity provider is down.
Two-step sign-in
Sign-in plus a 6-digit authenticator code; admins can require it, with 10 one-time backup codes.
Encrypted backup
A daily file of your database and settings, locked with a passphrase; the last 14 are kept.
Supervisor
An operator an admin has allowed to approve operating-state changes.
Audit trail
The record of who changed what and when: states, roles, approvals and settings.
